In a red envelope (红包, hóngbāo) the amount is part of the message. The right number says “prosperity,” “forever” or “I love you.” The wrong one can quietly offend.

Golden rules

  1. Prefer even amounts for happy occasions — good things come in pairs.
  2. Avoid 4 anywhere in the amount.
  3. Use new, crisp notes for paper envelopes.
  4. Let 8, 9 and 6 lead: 88, 99, 168, 188, 666, 888.
  5. Odd amounts are for condolences in many families (e.g. 101, 201, 501) — never use “lucky” festive numbers there.

Exact amounts depend on region, relationship and local income — in some places a wedding gift is expected to cover your share of the banquet. When in doubt, ask a family member.

Digital red packets

Mobile payment apps made red packets instant and playful. Couples often send amounts like 5.20, 13.14 or 52.0 throughout the day on May 20 — the messages are in the numbers.
