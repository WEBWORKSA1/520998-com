520 is a number that says “I love you.” Read aloud in Mandarin — wǔ èr líng — it sounds close to wǒ ài nǐ (我爱你). From that small coincidence grew one of the most commercially important love days in the world: May 20, “520 Day.”

Why 520 means “I love you”

Mandarin is full of near-homophones, and internet users have long turned digits into shorthand. In 520, the 5 (wǔ) stands in for 我 wǒ (I), the 2 (èr) for 爱 ài (love) and the 0 (líng) for 你 nǐ (you). It is not a perfect match — which is part of the charm. Typing three digits feels lighter than writing the words, so 520 became a shy, playful way to confess feelings in chats, comments and usernames.

Its sibling 521 works the same way, read either as another “I love you” or as 我愿意 wǒ yuànyì, “I’m willing” — the answer to a proposal.

Where 520 Day came from

520 Day has no ancient roots; it is a product of internet culture. A late-1990s Mandopop song that sang “five-two-zero means I love you” helped spread the code, and by the 2000s young people were marking 5/20 as a day to declare their feelings. Retailers noticed. Large e-commerce platforms began running 520 promotions in the 2010s, and the date steadily joined Valentine’s Day (February 14) and the traditional Qixi Festival as one of China’s three big love days.

The commercial impact is real. Chinese media reported, for example, that searches and purchases of “520” items on one major short-video shopping platform more than doubled year-on-year in the run-up to May 20, 2023, with perfume among the fastest-growing categories. Jewellers, florists, cosmetics brands, restaurants and travel companies now plan 520 campaigns months ahead.

How people celebrate

  • Gifts and flowers — roses (often 99 or 520 stems), jewellery, perfume and personalised items.
  • Red packets — sending a digital or paper red packet of 5.20, 52.0, 520 or 1314 yuan, where the amount itself carries the message.
  • Confessions — 520 is a socially accepted day to tell someone how you feel.
  • Marriage registrations — civil-affairs offices often see queues on May 20 and 21 because the date literally reads “I love you / I’m willing.”
  • Dates and dinners — set menus, couples’ packages and city light shows.

520 outside China

Chinese-speaking communities in Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore, Malaysia and the global diaspora use 520 too, and international brands selling to Chinese consumers now treat it as a seasonal moment. For non-Chinese speakers, 520 is an easy, meaningful “secret code” for a partner — as long as you explain it.

Tips for a great 520

  1. Let the number carry the message. A gift of 5.20, a 5:20 pm reservation or 52 tiny notes says more than price.
  2. Plan early. Flowers and restaurants book up; online promotions start up to two weeks before.
  3. Pair it with 1314. A card reading 5201314 turns a sweet gesture into a promise.
  4. Avoid 4 and 250 in amounts and quantities — see our number slang guide.
