Chinese speakers turn digits into words through sound. Once you know what each digit can “sound like,” codes such as 520, 1314 or 88 become instantly readable.

How number slang works

Every digit has a Mandarin name, and many of those names sound like common words. Writers pick whichever reading fits: 5 can be 我 (I), 2 can be 爱 (love), 9 can be 久 (long-lasting) or 就 (just), 8 can be 发 (prosper) or 拜 (bye). Context does the rest. That is why 88 means “bye-bye” in a chat but “double prosperity” on a price tag.

Numbers to avoid

Some codes are rude or unlucky. If you sell to, gift to or market to Chinese speakers, keep these out of prices, quantities, SKUs and red-packet amounts:

  • 4 on its own and in most combinations (sounds like 死, death). Many buildings skip 4th and 14th floors.
  • 250 — an insult meaning “idiot.”
  • 748 — “drop dead.”
  • 7456 — “I’m so angry.”

Love codes such as 1314, 3344 and 9420 are the famous exceptions where 4 reads as shì (world / is), so they are perfectly fine.
